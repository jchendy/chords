Every style, as it is and as it could be

Implemented: the app now plays everything proposed here (see js/styles.js); the left column is what it played before, kept so the change can still be heard.

Each style the practice tab offers is here twice: what the app plays now on the left, what is proposed on the right — the band and every guitar part, all playable. Under each is where to say keep it, take the proposal, mix, or drop it, and a box for anything you'd want different. New styles have their own cards (add / add with tweaks / skip), and the engine proposals are at the end. Decisions save as you go — to review/decisions.json when this page is served by serve.py, and to this browser either way; Copy decisions puts them on the clipboard.

Reading the proposals. Six bars each, as on the parts page: figure, fill, variant, fill, variant, fill — except that the proposed fills know whether the next bar changes chord (a change gets a walk or an approach, more of the same chord gets a push or a repeat), and the last bar of the form gets a turnaround where the style has one. "Needs:" on a card lists what the app's engine would have to learn for the part to sound this way there; here the page plays it.

The controls above apply to everything: the reading (proposed parts realise into the Chords, Pentatonic or Scales palette like the existing ones), techniques on or off, and three proposed engine features you can hear — timing and velocity humanized, slapback echo on the styles that live on it (rockabilly, surf, country), and the fill every four bars instead of two. Re-rolling changes which of the proposed fills each phrase gets.

Where the research came from. Each genre opens with what its players actually do, with the records that show it. Every name is a reference point for the style, never a source of lines; where a proposed sub-style is "inspired by" someone, it takes their way of playing in the terms a method book would use for it and nothing they own.
